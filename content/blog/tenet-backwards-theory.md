People call Tenet confusing. I think it is the simplest Nolan film, and that is exactly why it confuses everyone. My hypothesis: you are not supposed to follow the plot forwards. You are supposed to follow the entropy.

## The one rule that explains everything

Forget timelines. The film has exactly one rule: some things move backwards through time. A bullet that flies back into a gun. A car chase where one car un-crashes. Every "wait, what" moment is just this rule, applied consistently.

The trick that unlocked it: stop asking what happens next. Ask what already happened, viewed in reverse. The famous airport scene looks like chaos until you realize you are watching two directions of time share one hallway.

One crew moves forwards and plants things. The other crew moves backwards and un-plants them. Same hallway, same minute, opposite arrows. Once you see it, you cannot unsee it, and the scene becomes almost boring in how logical it is.

## My hypothesis: the Protagonist recruited himself

The film never shows who founded Tenet. My answer: the Protagonist did, from the future, and the whole film is him interviewing himself for the job.

Think about it. He gets briefed by people who know too much. He is tested in an opera siege that makes no sense until the end, when a masked soldier un-shoots someone to save him.

That soldier is him, inverted, closing his own loop. The grandfather paradox everyone worries about never fires because the loop was always closed. There is no first time. The film is a circle disguised as a line.

Nolan hides this the same way Interstellar hid its thesis: the answer is stated early and ignored. "Do not try to understand it. Feel it." That line is not a warning. It is the instruction manual. Understanding chases causes forwards. Feeling rides the entropy backwards.

## Why the backwards fights are genius, technically

Fighting someone who moves in reverse means every punch you throw has already landed from their view. The choreography had to be designed twice: once forwards, once as the exact mirror, then filmed so both read clearly.

No CGI shortcut exists for that. It is rehearsal as computation. People complain the sound mix buries the dialogue, and sure, but I watched it with subtitles and the mix stopped mattering. The images carry the information. The words were never the point.

## Entropy, explained like you are five

Everything falls apart over time. Ice melts. Rooms get messy. That direction, from tidy to messy, is entropy, and it is why time feels like it has an arrow. Now imagine something whose arrow points the other way: messy to tidy, un-breaking instead of breaking. From our side it looks like insanity. A bullet un-firing. A building un-exploding.

That is all Tenet's inverted objects are. They are not time traveling. They are just thermodynamically rude. And the film hands you a freebie in the title: TENET reads the same forwards and backwards. The movie tells you its own structure on the poster and trusts you will not notice until hour two.

> "Don't try to understand it. Feel it.", Tenet (2020). Terrible advice for physics. Perfect advice for this film.

## The simple version

Tenet is a palindrome wearing a tuxedo. Read it forwards and it fights you. Read it as two arrows sharing one hallway and it behaves. Nolan did not make a puzzle to frustrate you.

He made the only film that physically cannot be understood in one direction, about people learning the same lesson. The Protagonist stops asking what happens next around the same minute you do. That is the whole arc. That is the film.
