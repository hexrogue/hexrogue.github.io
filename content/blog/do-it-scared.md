I overthink everything. Small things get ten rehearsals in my head before I open my mouth. Big things get a full headcount of who will stand against me. Most days fear runs the meeting and I just sit there taking notes.

Took me years to get this one thing. The fear never leaves first. You do not wait until you feel brave and then act. You act while your hands shake. Nobody is brave. Everyone is afraid of something. Some people just move anyway.

## Nobody is brave

Bravery mostly lives in stories. Out here there is no fearless person. The speaker on stage, the guy asking questions in the meeting, the friend saying the unpopular thing. All scared. They stopped waiting for their nerves to give permission.

Once that clicked, fear got smaller for me. It stopped meaning I cannot do it. Now it is weather. Rain does not mean staying home forever, it means bringing an umbrella.

## "What will people say" is a prison

Most of my fear is one sentence. What will they think.

So what? You cannot please all people. Try and you end up pleasing nobody, yourself included. People judge anyway. Half of them are doing less than you. Their verdict was never the price of admission.

Do what is right even if the whole room stands against you. Easy to type, hard to live, I know. Standing alone feels terrible for a day. Living on other people's opinions feels terrible for years. I pick the shorter pain.

## Afraid to ask? Ask anyway

This one embarrasses me. I used to rather stay stuck than ask and look stupid. Directions, help, whatever everyone else seemed to already know. I would sit there lost instead of opening my mouth.

But what am I even afraid of? That someone thinks for three seconds that I did not know something. Then lunch happens and they forget. I stayed stuck for a week to protect three seconds of image. Terrible trade.

Just ask. Who cares. Worst case is a no, and a no is still information. Best case your problem dies in one sentence. Both beat sitting in silence.

## Shame is fear with an audience

Under most of my fear sits shame. Not scared of the thing itself. Scared of looking stupid doing it, with people watching. The dumb question nobody else asked. Trying something and failing where everyone can see.

I replay my shameful moments for a month. Everyone else forgot by lunch. People barely remember their own embarrassment. They definitely do not archive mine. And the ones who shame you for trying are always the ones trying nothing.

Shame fades every time you survive it. And you survive it every time. Ask the small question first. Let yourself be a beginner in public and each rep gets cheaper.

## Overthinking is fear with a notebook

Overthinking feels like preparing, but it is fear taking notes. Replaying conversations and inventing replies nobody will ever say, suffering ten versions of a thing that might take ten minutes. None of it happened.

So I shrink the thing until it fits in one move. Scared of the call? I write the first sentence down. Scared to post? I show one friend first. One real step kills a week of rehearsal.

## The replay does not stop after you act

You did it. Scared, but done. And now the second shift starts. Did I sound stupid. Should I have said it different. The event took ten minutes. The replay takes a week.

Same rule as before. The memory is always worse than the thing. Ask anyone who was there and they barely remember it. The replay feels like reviewing footage. It is actually just fear with editing software. Notice it, laugh if you can, and let the week pass. It always passes.

## Not all fear is wrong

I am not saying ignore every fear. Some of it is signal. Fear of real danger, real loss, a real cliff edge. That one earned its place. Listen to it.

The skill is telling signal from noise. Ask what the fear points at. A consequence you cannot undo, or embarrassment you will forget by lunch? The first one deserves respect. The second one gets an umbrella. Most of mine turn out to be the second kind.

## Do not borrow other people's fear

Sometimes the fear is not even yours. Family says do not. Friends say careful. It sounds like wisdom and sometimes it is. But often it is their fear wearing a suit, handed to you like advice.

Hear them out, then check the source. Have they done the thing? Or are they afraid of it too? Advice from someone who tried is worth gold. Caution from someone who never left the chair is just their weather report. Do not dress for someone else's rain.

## Playing small so nobody notices

Same fear, quieter version. Not failing in public. Never appearing. Not raising the hand, not showing the work, staying average on purpose so nobody looks at you too long.

It feels safe. It is just shame with better planning. Nobody can laugh at work they never saw. True. They also cannot use it or pay for it. Invisibility protects you from judgment and charges you everything else. If you are going to be scared either way, be scared somewhere visible.

## The one sentence version

You will never feel ready. Do it while the fear sits next to you. It can come along, it just does not get to drive.

> "Fear is not real. The only place that fear can exist is in our thoughts of the future.", After Earth (2013). Overthinking is exactly that. Thoughts of the future, rented as fact.
