I did not want to write this. I do not tell people my feelings. I feel ashamed even trying, like I am opening a door that should stay closed. But some things sit in the chest too long and start to ache, and this is one of them. So I am putting it here, for me. I doubt anyone will ever read this. Maybe years from now, an older me will find this page again and remember the nights he sat alone wondering. If he is the only reader this ever gets, that is enough.

The laptop clicks shut just past midnight. The fan winds down, the screen goes dark, and the house settles into the kind of silence that asks questions. I sit there for a while. I think about who I will marry.

There was a time I thought about it the way young men do. A face. A feeling. A heart beating faster. I will not pretend those things do not matter. But something shifted in me along the way, quietly, the way light changes in a room when the sun moves. I stopped asking who will make me happy. I started asking who I would trust with children who do not exist yet.

Because a wife is for me, and a mother is for them. They do not get a vote. The first world my children will ever know will be her. Her voice will teach them language before any teacher does. Her patience will teach them mercy before any book does. Her prayer, performed when she thinks no one is watching, will be the first worship they ever witness. Long before school, long before me, there is her. The old scholars called the mother the first school, and the older I get, the more that stops sounding like poetry and starts sounding like fact.

So the list I carry now is short, and it is the hardest list I have ever written. I want a woman whose faith I never have to wonder about. Whose tongue my children will be safe from. Whose heart can forgive, because anyone who marries me will need to forgive often. Beauty fades, and I am fading with it. Money comes and goes. But a woman who fears Allah will hold the home together on the days I cannot, tell my children the truth on the days I am wrong, and pull all of us back when we drift. Everybody drifts.

Time keeps moving whether I am ready or not. Friends marry one by one, and I mean it when I say I am happy for them. Then I come home to a quiet house, and the happiness sits beside something heavier. An empty chair across the table. A day full of small stories with nobody waiting to hear them. I tell myself I am fine. Most nights I almost believe it.

And underneath that, the fear I rarely say out loud. What if I choose wrong. What if I am not ready, not steady enough, not yet the man Islam asks a husband to be. Gentle when it costs him. Present when work pulls him. I know my own flaws the way you know the creaks in your own floorboards. Living with me will not be easy. Some nights I wonder if I am asking for someone good while I am still becoming someone good.

I know I am not perfect, and neither is she, whoever she is. I do not need perfect. I need someone willing to grow beside me while I grow beside her. Two unfinished people, choosing the same direction.

Life has hit me hard, and I am grateful for it now, though I was not grateful at the time. There were years that felt ugly and unfair, and no one could have convinced me then that those years were building anything. People say pain is what makes beauty, and it sounds like words on a mug until you live it. I did not understand it either. Then I turned twenty and looked back, and I finally saw what the hard years had been doing to me all along. They were making me into someone who could be trusted with more.

I do not know her name. Perhaps I have already met her and neither of us knows it yet. Perhaps she is somewhere living her own quiet nights, making the same du'a from the other side of a distance neither of us can measure. The thought steadies me more than I can explain.

Maybe three years. Maybe five. I have stopped trying to plan the how. I work on becoming a better man now, whether I have already met her or not. When the time comes, I want to get to know her the right way, with clear intentions and both families in the picture. After all of that, I leave the result to Allah. The effort is mine. The outcome was never mine to control.

There is a line at the end of Minecraft that stayed with me. It says you will be more disappointed by the things you did not do than by the ones you did. I carry that into this. I would rather spend these years becoming ready, and speak when the time comes, than sit still and wonder for the rest of my life what would have happened if I tried.

If I go first, I hope the home holds without me. I hope she tells our children who their father tried to be, honestly, flaws included. I hope they raise their hands and pray for me when I can no longer pray for myself. A man cannot ask for more than that.

I ask, and I wait, and I work. That is all I know how to do. Wherever she is tonight, I hope she is well. I will find her when the time is right.
